The GCSE prompting framework

A simple, memorable structure for writing effective prompts. Used across RES for Copilot for M365 and applicable to any AI tool. Think of it as giving your AI colleague a proper brief.

G
Goal
What do you want the AI to do?
Be specific about the task. Tell the AI exactly what output you need — a summary, a draft, a list, an analysis.
C
Context
Why do you need it, and who is involved?
Give background. Mention the project, the audience, the reason. The more context, the more relevant the response.
S
Source
Where should the AI look for information?
Point to specific files, emails, Teams chats, or web sources. In Copilot, this helps the AI search the right places in your M365 environment.
E
Expectations
How should the response look and sound?
Specify the tone, format, length, or level of detail. Do you want bullet points or prose? Formal or casual? Brief or comprehensive?

Example: the GCSE framework in action

Please generate 3-5 bullet points to prepare for a meeting with client X to discuss their "Phase 3" brand campaign. Focus on email and Teams chats since June. Please use only simple language so I can get up to speed quickly.
Goal Context Source Expectations
Tip: You don't always need all four components. A quick question might only need a Goal. But for important tasks, the more GCSE elements you include, the better your results will be.

Why does this matter?

AI tools work best when they understand what you need. A vague prompt gets a vague answer. Think of it like briefing a colleague — you wouldn't just say "write something about the project." You'd explain what you need, why, and how you want it delivered.

Copilot enhances your prompt through a process called grounding — it searches your M365 environment (emails, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive) to add relevant context. But the better your initial prompt, the more effectively grounding works.
